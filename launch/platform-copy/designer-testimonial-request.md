# Designer testimonial request

Phase 2 onwards, from Mon 5 October 2026 — and only to people who have actually used the library.

**The rule that governs this file:** we have no users at launch and we will not manufacture the
appearance of some. No "trusted by teams at". No logo wall. No quote from a friend who looked at
the website once. A testimonial is only usable if the person shipped something with it, and the
copy should say what they shipped.

---

## When to ask

Ask only when at least one of these is true:

- They have opened an issue or pull request that shows they used it in anger.
- They have posted a screenshot of the icons in a real interface.
- They replied to a launch post with a specific observation rather than a compliment.
- They asked a support question that only comes up mid-integration ("does the React package work in
  a server component?").

**Do not ask** someone who liked a post, starred the repository, or said "this is great". A
testimonial from someone who has not used the thing reads exactly like a testimonial from someone
who has not used the thing.

## What to ask for, in order of usefulness

1. **A screenshot of the icons in a real product.** Worth more than any sentence. One nav bar with
   a danfo in it beats a paragraph of praise.
2. **The specific thing they replaced.** "We were using a coach icon for our danfo route" is a
   story. "Great icons" is not.
3. **What they wanted and did not find.** This is a testimonial and a roadmap item at once, and
   people answer it more readily than they answer a request for praise.
4. **A quote, last.** And only if the first three produced something real.

---

## The message

Keep it short. A long request for a favour reads as a long favour.

**Subject:** `Did the icons hold up?`

---

Hello,

I saw you had the African Icon Library in [what you saw] — thank you for trying it.

I am putting together the project's case study and I would rather it contain real usage than
adjectives. Three questions, answer any or none:

1. **What did you replace?** I am specifically interested in what you were using before for that
   concept — a generic bowl, a coach for a danfo, that sort of thing.
2. **What did you look for and not find?** This is the one I want most. The backlog is public but
   it is our guess at priorities, not yours.
3. **Anything that broke?** Small sizes, dark themes, the React package, the Figma plugin.

If you are happy for it to be public, a screenshot of the icons in your interface would be worth
more to this project than a quote — and I would credit you and the product however you want, or
not at all.

If you would rather not, that is completely fine and I will not ask again.

Thank you,
Neustack Design
`icons@neustackstudio.com`

---

## The shorter version, for a reply in a thread

```
Thanks for trying it — would you be up for telling me two things? What you replaced, and what you
looked for and did not find. The second one is the useful one. Happy for it to stay private.
```

## How to use what comes back

### A quote

- **Verbatim.** No tidying, no "light editing for clarity", no removing the hedge. If a quote
  contains "the set is tiny but", the "but" stays in.
- **Attributed properly**, with role and product, and only with explicit permission for each. "A
  designer at a Lagos fintech" is acceptable if they asked for it; inventing that framing to make a
  quote sound more impressive is not.
- **Never a quote about the project's importance.** Quotes about what the icons did in a specific
  interface are usable. Quotes about representation and significance are flattering, unverifiable,
  and read as manufactured — even when they are completely sincere.

### A screenshot

- Get explicit permission for the specific image and the specific places it will be used.
- Check for anything unshippable in the frame — real customer data, unreleased features, a
  competitor's trademark.
- Credit the product and the person, unless they asked otherwise. Link to the product.
- Record it in [`../media-kit/credits.md`](../media-kit/credits.md) with the permission date.

### Criticism

Publish it. A case study containing "we wanted a `bold` weight and there isn't one" is more
credible than five compliments, and it is true — that weight does not exist and the copy says so
everywhere else already.

---

## What we will never do

- **Write a testimonial and ask someone to approve it.** If they have to be handed the words, they
  did not have them.
- **Use a logo wall.** Logos of companies who happen to have installed a package are not
  endorsements.
- **Say "trusted by" anything.** Trust is a claim about other people's states of mind and we have
  no way to check it.
- **Aggregate individuals into "the community".** One designer's opinion is one designer's opinion.
- **Solicit a review in exchange for anything.** Not swag, not a mention, not priority on an issue.
- **Ask twice.** One request. If there is no reply, that is the reply.

---

## If nobody replies

That is the expected outcome for a library that is a few weeks old, and it is not a problem to fix
with more asking.

Ship the case study without testimonials. Use the drawings, the grid, the validator output and the
status page — the project's evidence is technical, and a case study built on evidence you can check
is stronger than one built on quotes anyway.

Come back to this file in three months, when someone has actually shipped something.
